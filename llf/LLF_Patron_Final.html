Dear Friends,

Three years in, and there is a great deal to report. Faith in Crisis is in print, with a foreword by Rocco Buttiglione and an essay by Cardinal Robert Sarah. The Kydones Review — a peer-reviewed journal of ecumenical and interreligious studies — has received its first submissions. The Orientale Lumen Conference on Christian Unity is confirmed for July. The LLF Reading Circle launches at the end of May. Institutional grant applications are in the pipeline. The Substack, Tradition & Renewal, has grown past 1,200 readers — one essay found its way into a Boston College sacramental theology course, was noted by a member of the Dicastery for Divine Worship, and drew the attention of a cardinal. Roughly ten podcast appearances. It seemed like the right time to put something on paper for the people who made it possible.

Andrew Likoudis with Cardinal Christophe Pierre, Papal Nuncio to the United States, and his book Faith in Crisis.

My grandfather James Likoudis, whose name the Foundation carries, spent seventy years working toward the reunion of East and West. He never saw the full reunion. We hope to. The 1,000th anniversary of the Great Schism falls in 2054 — that is the horizon we are working toward.

Coming This Spring
New Edition · Emmaus Road
Ending the Byzantine Greek Schism
Third Revised Edition  ·  James Likoudis
Foreword by Scott Hahn

The definitive Catholic treatment of the theological questions dividing East and West, papal primacy and the Filioque, is coming in a new edition with a foreword by Scott Hahn, including the fourteenth-century Apologia of Demetrios Kydones for union with Rome. We expect it available by end of May.

Demetrios Kydones is the reason our journal carries the name it does. A fourteenth-century Byzantine statesman and theologian who taught himself Latin to read Aquinas, he translated the Summa into Greek and wrote a formal Apologia defending his reception into communion with Rome, addressed to his own emperor, in his own tongue, at real personal and political cost. The new edition includes his Apologia alongside St. Thomas Aquinas's Contra Errores Graecorum, and several chapters by Dr. Likoudis addressing the contemporary difficulties that continue to impede reunion and pointing toward their resolution. That is what the Foundation is for.

We have also met with the PR team at the St. Paul Center for Biblical Theology and meet with their Marketing team next week. They plan to do significant marketing when the book releases. The timing could not be better: ecumenism has been a defining focus of Pope Leo XIV's early pontificate, and that attention will only amplify interest in a rigorous, faithful treatment of the East-West divide. The more that marketing is running on its own momentum, the freer I am to do interviews, appear on podcasts, and use that visibility to introduce people to Dr. Likoudis's broader body of work. That is a real opportunity for the Foundation, and one we want to be ready for.

The Reading Circle

The LLF Reading Circle launches May 29 on Zoom, led by Lizbeth Moncada. First text: The Divine Primacy by James Likoudis. Biblical and epistemic foundations of papal primacy. Open to all.

Lizbeth brings remarkable gifts to this work — intellectual seriousness, a warm teaching presence, and a genuine passion for the Likoudis legacy. For a body of thought that has spent decades largely out of print and out of circulation, a community gathered to read through it carefully and discuss it is no small thing.

The Fellows Program: Planned, Not Yet Funded

The Fellows Program has been planned in real detail. Reading tracks are in place, a certificate program is being explored, and the scholarly questions it would address are clear. What it needs is funding for stipends and a program director who knows Dr. Likoudis's work deeply, and can direct fellows toward producing essays, translations, and digital media for an audience of 1,200 people who are already there and already engaged. That output is what makes the Foundation sustainable and attractive to grant-making organizations.

Your Support Compounds

What you have given has made a real difference. The Foundation has grown because of it: programs running, a journal taking shape, a conference confirmed, a community forming around work that deserves a wider audience.

I will be candid about where things stand. The Foundation has been, from the beginning, something I have run entirely in whatever spare time I have. It is not yet a paid career or a main job. It is something I believe in enough to do anyway. But we are reaching the point where that model has real limits.

The Kydones Review, the Reading Circle, the Orientale Lumen Conference, the publishing work, the Substack. Each one is a serious program. Together, they require more capacity than one person can provide in stolen hours. The next step is bringing in a part-time intern or administrative assistant to ensure things actually move on schedule. Your support makes it possible for me to compensate someone for their time, to stop doing everything alone, and to free myself to do what I do best: write, speak, and build the intellectual case for what the Foundation stands for.

I graduate in May. The window that opens afterward, with the conference, the book launch, the journal, the interview circuit, is the moment this all accelerates. I want to meet it with the capacity to do it well, not scrambling.

Whether you give $5, $50, $500, or $5,000, your gift makes all the difference.
