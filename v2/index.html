The moment we are in

A Church living through a crisis of trust

I.

An unmoored generation

Young adults are leaving in numbers — caught in a wider crisis of meaning, identity, and belonging in a post-Christian society, and unsure the Church they love can be trusted.

II.

The arguments turned inward

The objections that drove Lefebvre into schism in 1988 never disappeared. They migrated inward — circulating now among Catholics in communion with Rome who repeat the same suspicion of the pope, the Council, and the Mass.

III.

The road to 2054

The millennium of the Great Schism is approaching, and the work of reunion between Rome and Constantinople remains unfinished — three generations of one family's scholarship still unspent.

The faithful are left “like passengers in a storm-tossed boat — disoriented, unmoored, and struggling to trust in the Church's teaching, her mission, and the very foundations of a faith that once felt immovable.” — Faith in Crisis, Introduction

But the tradition is not the problem. The tradition has answers — and they are trustworthy.

Who I am
✦

I grew up inside this conversation. My grandfather, James Likoudis, spent his life answering the same questions I kept meeting online with a new intensity — questions about Vatican II, the reformed liturgy, and whether the pope could be trusted. He answered them with scholarly rigor and fidelity to the Magisterium. I've made it my work to carry that forward.

In August 2023 I began Faith in Crisis with no publisher, no budget, and no institutional backing — just a table of contents taking shape in my head and a willingness to cold-email professors, writers, and church officials across three continents. What I imagined as a modest ten-chapter volume grew into forty chapters and thirty-plus contributors, an imprimatur from Archbishop Lori, and a Foundation built from the ground up.

I bring that same institutional experience and editorial precision to Catholic publishers, nonprofits, and institutions who need to execute at a higher level.

— Andrew
